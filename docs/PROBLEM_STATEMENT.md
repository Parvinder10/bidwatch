# BIDWATCH: Problem Statement & Investigative Scope

## 1. Context & Background
Public procurement accounts for an estimated **15% to 30% of Gross Domestic Product (GDP)** in developing and emerging economies, representing trillions of rupees annually in public capital expenditure across transport, healthcare, municipal works, defense, and information technology. 

Despite statutory procurement frameworks such as the **General Financial Rules (GFR 2017)**, Central Vigilance Commission (CVC) guidelines, and electronic procurement portals (e.g., GeM, CPPP, State Portals), public procurement ecosystems are frequently exposed to structural vulnerabilities, market distortion, and administrative inefficiencies, including:
1. **Oligopolistic Vendor Capture**: A small syndicate or single contractor dominating specific departmental awards.
2. **Artificial Bidding Barriers**: Extremely short notice periods (e.g., < 7 days) and restrictive eligibility clauses designed to eliminate outside competition.
3. **Predatory Low-Bidding & Post-Award Escalation**: Bidders submitting artificially low prices to win tenders and subsequent reliance on post-award amendments/variations to inflate contract values by 30% to 100%.
4. **Single-Bidder Open Tenders**: Tenders nominally issued as "Open National Competitive Bidding" that receive only a solitary bid and are awarded without price benchmarking or retendering.
5. **Chronic Cancellation Loops**: Tenders cancelled upon receipt of bids from non-incumbents and retendered under tailored terms.

## 2. Core Problem Definition
Traditional public oversight relies heavily on post-facto manual audits conducted months or years after contract disbursement. Audit teams face severe analytical constraints:
- **Disparate, Unlinked Records**: Notice invitations, bid submissions, awards, and post-award contract amendments exist in fragmented files or unindexed tables.
- **Entity Identity Ambiguity**: The same corporate entity appears under dozens of unstandardized variations (e.g., abbreviations, typos, missing legal suffixes), masking true vendor concentration.
- **Lack of Objective Statistical Baselines**: Without quantitative baselines (such as the Herfindahl-Hirschman Index and multi-period escalation benchmarks), identifying anomalous patterns relies on ad-hoc whistleblower tips rather than systematic intelligence.

## 3. Objective & Investigative Scope of BIDWATCH
**BIDWATCH** is conceived not as an automated conviction mechanism, but as an **Investigative Intelligence and Decision Support Platform** for auditors, oversight commissions, vigilance officers, and data journalists.

### Explicit Analytical Boundaries:
- **Identify Patterns, Not Guilt**: The platform flags anomalies that deviate significantly from empirical baselines and statutory guidelines. Every flagged lead represents an *evidentiary starting point for human audit*, not an accusation of corruption.
- **Explainability as a Core Tenet**: Black-box machine learning scores are deliberately avoided. Every analytical flag provides full transparency: what was observed, which exact database fields support it, why it was flagged, the numerical baseline comparison, and a human audit checklist.
- **Holistic Lifecycle Coverage**: The investigative scope encompasses the complete procurement lifecycle: Notice -> Bidding Competition -> Contract Award -> Post-Award Execution & Corrigenda.
